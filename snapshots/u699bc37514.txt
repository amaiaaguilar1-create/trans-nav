Sports Medicine
Boise Clinics
Sports medicine is an area of medical practice which provides treatment of injuries resulting from athletic activities. A physician practicing sports medicine focuses on sports-related medical services. This may include preventative measures such as conditioning and injury prevention, as well as treatments such as osteopathic manipulation, rehabilitation, or injections.

Tuberculosis (TB)
Boise Clinics
The TB clinic in Boise provides evaluation and treatment for patients who may have been exposed to tuberculosis or who have a positive TB test.
TB screening is recommended by the USPSTF for at-risk populations:
https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/Page/Document/RecommendationStatementFinal/latent-tuberculosis-infection-screening
There are two different kinds of tests that can be used to detect TB bacteria in the body: the tuberculin skin test (TST, or often called PPD), and TB blood tests (IGRAs).

HIV
Boise Clinics
The Wellness Center at our Emerald Clinic in Boise is a Ryan White grant recipient clinic that provides comprehensive, interdisciplinary medical care to more than 800 individuals living with HIV/AIDS in Southern Idaho and Eastern Oregon. Over the last two decades, the Wellness Center has grown to become the largest clinic in Idaho treating persons living with HIV/AIDS, while also providing nationally recognized graduate medical education opportunities in HIV care.
We see patients who are newly diagnosed with HIV and who have been living with HIV for years, while also providing a wide range of consult services on HIV-related issues.

PrEP
Boise and Nampa Clinics
Pre-exposure prophylaxis, or PrEP, is a way for people who do not have HIV to help prevent HIV infection by taking a pill every day. When used consistently, PrEP has been shown to greatly reduce the risk of HIV infection in people who are at substantial risk.
PrEP is a powerful HIV prevention tool, and can be combined with condoms and other prevention methods to provide even greater protection than when used alone. People who use PrEP must commit to taking the drug daily and seeing their health care provider every 3 months for HIV testing and other follow-up.
