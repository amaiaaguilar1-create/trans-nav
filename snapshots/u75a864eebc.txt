Instructions and Forms for Petition for Waiver of Filing
Fees
(Based on inability to pay)
*For Civil Actions, or Civil Cases, and Small Claims Court Cases only*
ND Legal Self Help Center Staff and Court employees can’t help you fill out forms, or create
documents for you. If you’re unsure how to proceed, consult a lawyer.
ND Legal Self Help Forms aren’t official forms. Judges and courts aren’t required to accept
them. There’s no guarantee Center forms will be accepted. Use at your own risk.
Don’t include these instruction sheets when you serve or file the completed Waiver of Filing
Fee forms.
The Waiver of Filing Fee forms can be used if you’re unable to pay the filling fee. Fill out the
forms as completely and accurately as possible. The judge or judicial referee decides whether
you qualify for waiver of filing fees.
The Filing Fee Waiver doesn’t include waiver of the following:
• Transcript fees;
• Copy fees;
• Certified copy fees; or
• Cost of serving documents.
Type your answers or print neatly using dark ink. (Hand-written forms must be readable.)
Consult a lawyer if you don’t know how to answer the questions on these forms.
Step One:
Fill Out the Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees (Form 1)
Fill out the “Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees” form (Form 1) included in this packet. This form
asks the court to permit you to file your case without paying the required filing fee.
Caption (Top of Form):
 Fill in the name of the county where your case is located and the civil or Small Claims case
number, which is also called the “Civil No”.
• On the line marked “Plaintiff/Petitioner”, fill in the name of the Plaintiff/Petitioner.
• On the line marked “Defendant/Respondent”, fill in the name of the
Defendant/Respondent.

Paragraph 1:
 If you’re the Plaintiff/Petitioner in the case, put a () in the “Plaintiff/Petitioner”
checkbox. If you’re the Defendant/Respondent in the case, put a () in the
“Defendant/Respondent” checkbox.
Paragraph 2:
 Review carefully.

 Date and sign the Petition.
 Complete the lines following your signature.
Step Two:
Fill Out the Financial Declaration in Support of Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees
(Form 2)
Fill out the “Financial Declaration in Support of Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees” form (Form 2)
included in this packet. This form tells the Court about your financial circumstances and why it’s
a hardship for you to pay the filing fee.
Caption (Top of Form):
 Fill in the top of the form the same way you filled in the top of your Petition for Waiver of
Filing Fees form (Form 1) in Step 1.
Paragraph 1:
 Fill in your full name in the space provided.
Paragraph 2 – Public Assistance:
 Check () the first box if you receive public assistance.
• If you receive public assistance, check () the box(es) indicating the type(s) of
public assistance you receive. If you check () Other, indicate on the line the
type of public assistance you receive that isn’t included above.

Paragraph 3 – Assets:
 In the first table, tell the Court the current amounts of:
• Cash you have on hand;
• Money deposited in financial institutions;
• Stocks and bonds; and
• Total amounts of cash, money deposited in financial institutions, stocks and bonds.
 In the second table on Page 1:
• List all other assets, each asset’s approximate value, and who is in possession of the
asset.
• Total the values of each asset you list.
• Attach additional sheets if necessary and indicate that you have done so on the
form.
• At the end of Paragraph 3, provide the court with a total of the value of all of your
assets listed in the two tables on the bottom of Page 1 and the top of Page 2.
Paragraph 4 – Liabilities:
 Tell the court about your debts. List each debt separately, naming the creditor, the
unpaid balance and the monthly payment.
• Attach additional sheets if necessary and indicate that you’ve done so on the form.
• At the end of Paragraph 4, provide the court with a total amount of all of your debts.
Paragraph 5 – Income:
 Tell the court about your monthly income and your spouse’s monthly income. Write an
amount for each category, even if the amount is “0”. (You may find the information
requested on a pay stub, W-2 form, or your most recent income tax return.)
• If you don’t have a spouse, type or write “N/A” in each category in the “Spouse’s
Income” row.
• List all deductions taken from your pay check or gross income. If you don’t have a
deduction for health insurance, for example, place a “0” on that line.
• For “Total Deductions,” provide the total of all of the deductions.
• For “Net Income,” subtract the “Total Deductions” total from the “Gross
Income” total.
• At the end of Paragraph 5, tell the court how often you’re paid. Do the same for
your spouse. If you don’t have a spouse, type or write “N/A.”

Paragraph 6 – Expenses:
 Tell the court about your monthly expenses, and the expenses of other members of your
household.
• Write an amount you spend each month for the category provided. If you don’t have
an expense for a category, type or write "0”.
• At the end of Paragraph 5, provide the court with a total of your monthly expenses.
Paragraph 7 - Date and Signature (Read Paragraph 7 carefully!):
By signing your name, you’re telling the Court that everything you included in this Declaration is
true and correct, and you have a good faith reason for your requests. If you’re not telling the
truth, if you’re misleading the Court, or if you’re filing this Declaration for an improper purpose,
the Court could find you in contempt or you may be prosecuted for perjury.
 Fill in the date, name of the City, County, State and Country where you’re physically
located when you sign the Declaration.
• Sign your name.
• Fill in your printed name, address, telephone number, and email address.
Step Three:
Fill Out the Order Waving Filing Fees (Form 3)
Fill out the “Order Waiving Filing Fees” form (Form 3) included in this packet. This is your
proposed Order for the judge or judicial referee to sign. If the judge or judicial referee grants
your Petition and waives the filing fee, they sign an Order. If the judge or judicial referee grants
your Petition, they also decide whether to use your proposed Order.
Caption (Top Part of Form):
 Fill in the top of the Order Waiving Filing Fees form (Form 3) the same way you did on
your Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees form (Form 1) in Step 1.
Paragraph 1:
 If you’re the Plaintiff/Petitioner in the case, put a () in the “Plaintiff/Petitioner”
checkboxes. If you’re the Defendant/Respondent in the case, put a () in the
“Defendant/Respondent” checkboxes.
Don’t sign this form. Go to step 4.

Step Four:
Makes Copies of Completed Forms
Make one copy each of the completed “Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees" (Form 1) and completed
"Financial Declaration in Support of Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees" (Form 2). Keep the copy of
each form for yourself.
Step Five:
File the Original Forms with the District Court
Take the original of the “Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees" form (Form 1), the "Financial Declaration
in Support of Petition for Waiver of Filing Fees" (Form 2), and the "Order Waving Filing
Fees" (Form 3) to the Clerk of Court’s Office in the county where your civil case or Small Claims
Court case is located.
Tell the Clerk of Court that you wish to file the documents. You may file the forms at the same
time you file your documents that generate the filing fee.
Step Six:
Wait to See How the Judge or Judicial Referee Decides Your Petition
The court notifies you if you can proceed without paying the filing fees by sending a signed copy
of an Order to you.
If the judge or judicial referee grants your Petition and uses your proposed Order, you receive a
copy of the completed and signed Order Waiving Filing Fees form. If the judge or judicial referee
grants your Petition, but doesn’t use your proposed Order, you receive a copy of their Order.
If the judge or judicial referee grants your Petition, your documents are filed without paying the
filing fees.
If the judge or judicial referee doesn’t grant your Petition, you receive a copy of the Order
denying your Petition. You must pay the filing fees before your documents can be filed in the
case.

Lawyer Resources and Limited Legal Representation
You’re not required to hire a lawyer to bring a civil case in North Dakota State District Court. If
you decide to represent yourself, you must follow all of the rules, laws and procedures a lawyer
is required to follow.
Lawyer Resources
If you want to find a lawyer to represent you, you may find the following options of interest.
• Legal Services of North Dakota is a non-profit organization, providing free legal
assistance to North Dakota residents in a variety of matters based on income. Legal
Services of North Dakota can also determine whether an applicant meets the income
requirements for the Volunteer Lawyers program that offers low-cost legal assistance
based on income. The phone number is (800) 634-5263 and the website is lsnd.org.
• The State Bar Association provides a lawyer referral service to match paying clients in
need of legal services with lawyers. The phone number is (866) 450-9579 and the
website is sband.org. The cost is $30.00 for a 30 minute consultation with a lawyer.
• Dakota Plains Legal Services is a non-profit legal services organization that provides free
legal assistance to low-income individuals, older Americans and veterans. Dakota Plains
Legal Services (DPLS) has eight offices and serves communities across South Dakota and
North Dakota, including nine tribal nations. DPLS is committed to increasing access to
justice with quality legal assistance. Contact information is available on the DPLS
webpage of dpls.org.
• For a list of all lawyers who are licensed to practice in North Dakota, go to the North
Dakota Supreme Court website at ndcourts.gov/Lawyers.
Limited Legal Representation
Lawyers licensed to practice in North Dakota may provide Limited Legal Representation in civil
actions. Limited Legal Representation (sometimes called “unbundling”) is a way that a lawyer
can help you with part of your case while you do the rest of your case. You pay for the part of
the case the lawyer handled.
For example:
• You may want a lawyer to give you an expert opinion about your options, or your legal
rights and responsibilities;

• You can consult with a lawyer to prepare or review your paperwork, but attend hearings
yourself;
• You can represent yourself through the whole case, and periodically consult with a
lawyer who can coach you on the law, procedures and strategy;
• You can do the preparation yourself and hire a lawyer just to make the court
appearance for you.
You and the lawyer must agree in writing to Limited Legal Representation.
North Dakota Free Legal Answers
This civil legal program is a partnership of the American Bar Association and the State Bar
Association of North Dakota.
The purpose of the program is to provide free answers to specific civil legal question to low-
moderate income North Dakotans who submit their questions online. Anonymous volunteer
lawyers answer your question, but can’t represent you.
Go to nd.freelegalanswers.org/ for information about the program, the online application, and,
if you qualify, ask your civil legal question.
Please note that this program doesn’t provide any assistance with criminal legal questions.
