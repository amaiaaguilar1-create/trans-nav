Two prominent Massachusetts health organizations have ceased providing certain gender-affirming medical services to transgender youth, citing federal policy changes and funding pressures under the current administration.
Fenway Health, a leading Boston-based LGBTQ+ health center, announced in October 2025 that it would no longer offer hormones or puberty blockers to patients under 19, effective October 1, due to revised federal requirements from the Department of Health and Human Services that prioritize other goals and threaten grant funding for such care.
Shortly after, Outer Cape Health Services, which operates clinics across Cape Cod and the Islands, confirmed it would halt gender-affirming care—including puberty blockers and hormone therapy—for patients under 19 to protect its federal funding.

These decisions mark notable shifts in Massachusetts, a state long viewed as supportive of transgender health care, amid broader national trends where dozens of hospitals and clinics have limited or ended similar services for minors.
The moves have drawn criticism from advocates and patients, who describe them as devastating for young people relying on these treatments. Fenway Health and Outer Cape Health Services continue to provide other services, including adult care and mental health support.
No other Massachusetts hospitals or systems were specifically named in connection with ending youth transgender surgeries in the original report referenced, though recent developments indicate ongoing pressures in the field.
