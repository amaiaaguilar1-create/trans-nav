New Orleans Health Department
Your partner in community health, offering essential health care access, family support programs, and emergency services. We provide comprehensive care through health care facilities, nutrition programs, and preventive services.
Our focus includes public health emergencies, chronic disease management, behavioral health support, and environmental initiatives - all working together to create a healthier New Orleans.
Updated Respiratory Virus Immunization Recommendations
This reference includes the current flu, COVID-19 and RSV immunization recommendations from the American Academy of Family Physicians, American Academy of Pediatrics, American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, and the Infectious Diseases Society of America into a single source. For more detailed guidance from these groups, visit nola.gov/vaccines .
Influenza (flu)
COVID-19
Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)
Children and Adolescents 0-18 years
Annual flu vaccine for all children 6 months+
All young children ages 6-23 months and children 2-18 years in certain risk groups or if desired
All infants <8 months, if no protection from RSV vaccine during pregnancy and children 8-19 months in certain risk groups
Pregnancy
Annual flu vaccine (avoid nasal spray flu vaccine)
Annual updated COVID-19 vaccine
One-time RSV vaccine in first eligible pregnancy if 32-36 weeks pregnant, given between September 1 and March 1
Adults 19-64 years
Annual flu vaccine for all adults ages 19+
Annual updated COVID-19 vaccine
One-time RSV vaccine for adults ages 50-74 at increased risk of severe RSV
Older Adults 65+ years
Annual flu vaccine for all adults, enhanced flu vaccine (high dose, adjuvanted, recombinant, or mRNA) for 65+, if possible
Annual updated COVID-19 vaccine for adults 65+, followed by a second dose 6 months later to boost protection
One-time RSV vaccine recommended for adults ages 50-74 at increased risk and all adults age 75+
Immunocompromised
Annual flu vaccine for those ages &ge;6 months (avoid nasal spray flu vaccine)
Annual updated COVID-19 vaccine for those ages &ge;6 months
Single dose of RSV vaccine for those ages&ge;18 years and for those ages <18 years, administration should be guided by shared decision making
Healthcare Personnel
Annual flu vaccine
Annual updated COVID-19 vaccine
Follow age- and risk-based RSV vaccine recommendations

New Orleans Health and Human Services Releases New Guidance to Expand Access to Doxy PEP, an Affordable Tool for Preventing Bacterial STIs
NEW ORLEANS - As Louisiana continues to face some of the highest rates of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), the New Orleans Office of Health and Human Services (NOHHS) is encouraging residents and health care providers to learn about doxycycline postexposure prophylaxis (doxy PEP). This evidence-based strategy can help reduce the risk of certain bacterial STIs after a potential exposure. To support the community and strengthen local prevention efforts, NOHHS is releasing a doxy PEP guidance designed to help both residents and providers understand how doxy PEP works, who may benefit from it, and how it can be used as part of routine sexual health care. This resource aims to make conversations about doxy PEP easier, improve access to the medication, and contribute to lowering STI rates in New Orleans.

New Orleans Department of Health and Human Services Unveils Three-Year Impact Report Showcasing Community-Driven Health Improvements
NEW ORLEANS &mdash; The New Orleans Department of Health and Human Services (NOHHS) just released its 2022&ndash;2025 Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP) Impact Report, offering a look at three years of collective work to strengthen health and well‑being across the city. Powered by the energy and expertise of hundreds of community partners, the report shows how public agencies, healthcare providers, nonprofits, educators, and residents joined forces to take on some of New Orleans' toughest health challenges together.

New Orleans Mobile Crisis Team Delivers Faster, Smarter Care for Behavioral Health Emergencies
New Orleans Mobile Crisis Intervention Unit (MCIU) dispatches mental health professionals who de-escalate, assess, stabilize, and connect residents to care right in the community, often avoiding hospitalization entirely. This team has been able to operate as the fourth arm of the City's first responder system to provide rapid response to behavioral health calls where weapons or violence are not involved.

New Orleans Department of Health and Human Services and March of Dimes Promote Low Dose Aspirin to Reduce Preeclampsia Risk
NEW ORLEANS &mdash; The New Orleans Department of Health and Human Services, in partnership with March of Dimes, announces the expansion of its preeclampsia awareness campaign, &ldquo;Take It Easy, Baby!&rdquo;. Designed to combat one of the leading causes of maternal and infant mortality and morbidity, the campaign continues to increase awareness about the signs and symptoms of preeclampsia in Orleans Parish. A new provider education component of the campaign is now available to educate healthcare professionals on the benefits of low dose aspirin.

City of New Orleans Department of Health and Human Services Releases 2025 Annual Opioid Report
NEW ORLEANS &mdash; The New Orleans Department of Health and Human Services released its 2025 Annual Opioid Report, revealing a 4% decrease in accidental overdose deaths across Orleans Parish compared to 2024, building on a significant downward trend that began in 2023. While this decline&mdash;from 293 to 282 fatalities&mdash;marks continued progress, the report underscores that overdose deaths remain significantly higher than pre‑pandemic levels.
