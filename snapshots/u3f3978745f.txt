Notice of Increased Processing Time:
The New Hampshire Division of Vital Records Administration is currently experiencing a higher-than-usual volume of vital records requests, which has resulted in longer processing times. We apologize for any inconvenience this may cause.
At this time, most vital records requests are being completed within approximately 90 business days from the date they are received. Genealogical requests may require up to 120 business days due to the additional research involved. Before canceling or stopping payment on a check, please contact the Division of Vital Records to confirm whether your request has been received and/or processed. Canceling or stopping payment without verification may result in returned-check fees. Please know that the Division is working diligently to reduce processing times.
It is important to know that the following New Hampshire vital records can be purchased from any New Hampshire City or Town Clerk’s office, regardless of where the event occurred. Processing times vary by municipality.
Birth: 1935 to Present
Marriage: 1960 to Present
Death: 1965 to Present
Divorce: 1979 to within 6 months of the present
Thank you for your patience and understanding as we work to improve our processing times and continue providing the highest level of service to the public.
Amending and Correcting Records
Vital records may contain errors, typos, or missing information. In some cases, records must be amended to reflect a legal name change or updated parentage information. Below is general guidance on how to correct or amend a vital record. Please note that not all situations are covered here, and additional documentation or different procedures may be required. Customers are encouraged to contact the Division of Vital Records or the appropriate town or city clerk for further assistance.
Who Can Amend a Certificate?
Eligibility to amend a record varies depending on the type of event and is defined by state law.
• Birth Records : May be amended by a parent, the registrant if 18 or older, or a legal guardian/representative. Proof of guardianship or representation is required.
• Marriage Records : May be amended by either party or the officiant. Officiants cannot amend demographic information about the parties, and parties may not amend ceremony details without a notarized letter from the officiant.
• Death Records : Demographic information may be amended by the funeral director, the original informant, or next-of-kin as defined in RSA 290:16. Requests from next-of-kin may take several weeks, as the clerk must notify the informant by law.
• Divorce Records : May only be amended by the court where the divorce occurred.
Amending Genealogical Records
Records classified as open to the public (genealogical records) may only be amended if:
• The individual is still living and it is their birth or marriage record, or
• The record is needed for a legal purpose and proof of that purpose is provided,
• And the requester meets the requirements under “Who Can Amend a Certificate.”
Public genealogical records include:
• Births: 100 years or older
• Marriages: 50 years or older
• Deaths: 50 years or older
Where Are Amendments Initiated?
Amendments to birth and death certificates (VSX, VSCR, or court-ordered) must be initiated with the clerk of the town or city where the event occurred. Amendments to marriage certificates must be initiated with the clerk of the town or city where the marriage intentions were filed.
How to Apply
The amendment process depends on the age of the record, calculated from the date the record was filed. Amendments may be submitted in person or by mail. Contact the appropriate town or city clerk for specific instructions. A $10.00 fee applies.
VSX (Within Six Months of Filing):
Most corrections within the first six months can be made using a VSX form. Additional documentation may be requested in some cases. VSX corrections are typically completed the same day.
VSCR (After Six Months):
Corrections made after six months require a notarized VSCR form and supporting documentation. Processing may take up to 15 business days.
Documentation Required for VSCR
All VSCR amendments must include two pieces of documentary evidence dated as close to the event as possible. Acceptable documents may include, but are not limited to:
• Baptismal Records
• Hospital Records
• Early School Records
• Census Records
• Income Tax Records
• Vital Records
• Employment Records
• Voter Registration Cards or Lists
• Application for Insurance Policies
• Copies from Family Bible Records
• Military Records
• Newspaper clippings, such as birth or wedding announcements
Original documents should be presented when applying in person; clerks will make photocopies and return the originals. When applying by mail, notarized copies may be submitted, except for vital records, which must be certified originals. All original documents will be returned to the requester.
Correction of Names and Adding Names
Occassionally, the name which appears on a person’s certificate is a different spelling or not the name by which they are commonly known. In this case I is necessary to determine whether the difference is due to an error or whether the person’s name has changed by usage.
Within the first six months from the date the record was filed, typos can be corrected using a VSX form. (Please note, substantial or complete changes to a child’s name will require a court ordered legal name change.)
Once the first six months have passed, typos can be corrected using a VSCR form, which requires notarization and two pieces of documentary evidence to support the amendment. The two pieces must be from the early years of the individual, usually with the first five years of life. If adequate documentary evidence is not submitted with the VSCR, it will then be necessary to obtain a legal name change by court order.
Upon receipt of a certified copy of a court order changing the name of a person born in New Hampshire and upon request of such person, the town or city clerk shall amend the certificate of birth to reflect the new name. “A.K.A” shall be noted in the marginal note and both names shall appear on the face of the amended record.
Some older records do not show the given name of the child or may display the child’s name as simply “baby boy” or “baby girl.” Unless the record is a public record (over 100 years) a certified copy of such a certificate cannot be issued until the name is added.
Affidavits of Paternity (Voluntary)
In most cases, a voluntary Affidavit of Paternity may be used to add a father to a birth certificate for a child under 18. Both parents must sign the form in the presence of a notary public. The form is filed with the clerk in the town or city where the child was born. Additional documentation may be required in some circumstances. Affidavit of Paternity forms are available from those town or city clerk’s offices and can be mailed to parents upon request.
Court-Ordered Amendments
Changes to names, sex, and parentage require a certified court order. New Hampshire honors most out-of-state court orders; however, general parentage or custody orders, DNA results, and out-of-state voluntary parentage forms cannot be used to add a father to a birth certificate.
Common New Hampshire court orders include:
• Legal Name Change Order
• Legal Gender Change Order
• Uniform Support Order (for adding a father)
• Court-Ordered Affidavit of Paternity (for adding a father)
This information is intended as general guidance; additional procedures or documentation may be required.
