General Information
This guide is designed to help transgender, nonbinary, and gender non-
conforming individuals ages eighteen and older to navigate the legal name
change process in Erie County, New York.
It includes information about seeking a legal name change pro se (without the
help of an attorney), changing your legal name on government-issued
documents, changing your gender marker on government-issued documents,
and changing your name and gender marker on other documents.
This guide has been updated as of March 1, 2025, to reflect recent changes in
federal policies regarding gender markers. Please note that information may
be out of date as polices are changing rapidly. Please refer to the other
resources listed and linked throughout this guide for updated information but
do not rely on them for legal advice.
Use the Table of Contents above to locate the relevant information. If you are
stuck, confused, or overwhelmed, or are denied services while attempting to
change your name or correct your documents, please contact Neighborhood
Legal Services, Inc. at (716) 847-0650.
Learn more at https://nls.org/

New Federal Policies (2025)
Executive Order 14168
The White House, on January 20, 2025, issued Executive Order 14168. It prohibits
individuals from changing their gender markers or “sex designation” with any federal
agency. As of March 1, 2025, any application seeking any kind of gender marker change
with the federal government had been suspended.
U.S. Passports
As of March 1, 2025, the State Department is no longer recognizing gender marker “X”
and is no longer accepting passport renewals that request a gender marker change.
However, the State Department is still processing name change requests for passports.
All currently issued passports, including those with a gender marker “X” or those listing a
gender marker different from a person’s “sex designation” at birth, will remain valid for
travel until their listed expiration date.
If you submit a new passport application and have any identification documents that do
not list matching gender markers, it is likely that your application will be denied.
Please refer to the Department of State’s website for updated policies.
U.S. Social Security Administration
As of March 1, 2025, the Social Security Administration (SSA) is not processing any gender
marker changes. However, the SSA is still processing legal name changes.
Please refer to the Social Security Administration's website for updated policies
New York Law
The Gender Recognition Act
The Gender Recognition Act became law in New York State in June 2021, although not all
sections were effective until June 24, 2022. The Act made several changes to the law,
including:
5
“X” Gender Markers
The Gender Recognition Act allows New Yorkers to choose "M", “F”, or “X” as a gender
marker on New York State-issued birth certificates, driver’s licenses, and non-driver
identification cards.
Self-Attestation Standard
Under the Gender Recognition Act, New York now has a “self-attestation” standard
regarding gender markers on State-issued documents. This means that you can check a
box to self-select a gender marker. You do not need a doctor’s note, medical testimony, a
court order, a signed affidavit, or any other type of supporting documentation.
For gender markers on documents issued outside of New York State, New York has
implemented a new process to “petition for change of sex designation.” This process has
been added to the petition process for changing your name, meaning the same form is
submitted to change both your legal name and your “sex designation.” An order from a
court granting a sex designation change is not required to change the gender markers on
any documentation issued by New York State. This process is discussed in further detail in
the “Gender Marker Change” section of this guide.
Removal of Publication Requirement
The Gender Recognition Act eliminated the publication requirement for all name changes.
This means someone who wants to change their name does not need to publish a notice
in a local newspaper.
Name Changes on a Child’s Birth Certificate
The Gender Recognition Act now allows parents to change their own name on a child’s
birth certificate and identify themselves as “mother,” “father,” or “parent.”
Legal Name Change Process
Overview
When changing your legal name or requesting a court order granting a sex designation
change, you will need to submit certain documents with the Erie County Supreme Court
at the Erie County Clerk’s Office. If you do not live in Erie County, you will need to file
your documents listed below with the Supreme Court in the county where you live.
6
Submitting the required documents can be done in person or by online filing (e-filing).
After you have filed the necessary documents, a judge will review your case. The judge
will contact you if more information is needed regarding your name change or sex
designation change. Occasionally, the judge may require someone seeking a name or
gender marker change to appear in court, depending on the circumstances of your case.
If the judge approves your name change or sex designation change, you will receive a
signed “Order Granting Name Change and/or Sex Designation Change for Individual
Adult” from the judge. After you receive the signed Order, you will then need to file it
with the Erie County Clerk.
After you have done this, your name change will be complete. You need the Order to
change the name on most of your documentation, such as your driver’s license or
passport. You may also need the Order to change the gender marker on some
documents, if applicable. As of March 1, 2025, the federal government is not permitting
gender marker changes on any federally issued documents. However, for documents
issued by New York State, you do not need an Order. For New York State-issued
documents, you can simply identify your gender yourself, known as the “self-attestation
standard.”
It is generally a good idea to get multiple certified copies of the Order and keep an extra
certified copy in your records. You can obtain certified copies from the Erie County Clerk.
Forms
Name change forms are available at:
www.nycourts.gov/courthelp/namechange/forms.shtml
Required Documents
The first step in seeking a legal name change is filing the appropriate documents with the
court. You will need three forms:
 Petition: “Name Change and/or Sex Designation Change Petition for Individual
Adult,” Form USC-NC1. (“Petition”)
 Order: “Order Granting Name Change and/or Sex Designation Change for
Individual Adult,” Form USC-NC01. (“Order”)
 Request for Judicial Intervention, Form US-840. (“RIJ”)
Complete these documents electronically or, if handwritten, printed neatly in black ink.
Computer-assistance: The courts have a computer program that will help you to fill out
these forms, called the Adult Name Change Petition Program. If you are 18 years of age or
7
older and you are not in prison, on probation, or on parole, you may use the New York
Courts Name Change Petition Program to fill out the necessary forms for a name change.
This will not file the forms but will fill in the forms for you. To use it, visit the court
website and click on the form (these forms say “Type: DIY” (“Do It Yourself”). The link is
also at the end of this Guide under “Additional Resources.”
You will also need:
 Original or certified copy of your birth certificate
o If you were born in New York and no birth certificate is available, then a
certificate from the Commissioner of the local Board of Health saying that
no birth certificate is available will work
o If you were born outside of the United States, you can provide a certificate
or record of birth
 Court fee of $210 (The fee can be waived if you cannot afford it.)
o To waive the fee, fill out and file these two forms. Both of these need to be
notarized. That means they must be signed in front of a New York notary
public. Do not sign these forms until you are in front of a notary public.
 Notary: You must have identification to notarize forms.
o Many banks, UPS stores, courts, and county clerk offices have notaries.
Some notaries charge a fee.
 Optional Fee Waiver Forms:
o “Poor Person Order” (which may allow you to waive fees)
o Affidavit in Support of Application to Proceed as a Poor Person
Petition
A petition is an application that asks the court to start your name change case. The
petition must include specific information about you, such as:
o Your birth or other legal name and any previous names you have used.
o Your correct chosen name.
o Your address.
o Your birthday and the city you were born in.
o Why you are seeking a name change (this reason must be legitimate and non-
fraudulent, meaning that you are not changing your name to commit fraud).
o If you were convicted of a crime, information about the crime, and time served.
You can attach a copy of the Certificate of Incarceration or Certificate of
Disposition to your Petition.
o If you were convicted of bankruptcy, when the judgment was made, and the
terms. You can attach a copy of the judgment along with the Petition.
o If there are judgments or liens against you, when the judgment was made, who the
judgment is owed to, and the amount of the judgment. A judgment is when a court
8
declares that you owe a debt to someone. A judgment shows up on your credit
report. A lien happens when you owe a debt to someone and a legal claim for that
amount is put on your income or property until that debt is paid back. If you have
judgments or liens against you or your property, you can attach copies.
o If you are involved in a lawsuit, give the names of the people in the lawsuit, the
reason for the lawsuit, the Index Number or Docket Number, and what court the
lawsuit is in. You can attach copies of the court papers to your Petition.
o If you pay child or spousal support and how much. You can attach a copy of the
support order.
Notary: You must sign (your current legal name) and date the petition before a notary.
Order
An order is a command from a judge. An Order Granting Name Change and/or Sex
Designation Change will allow you to change your documentation to reflect your correct
name on certain documents. It certifies that your name has been changed and/or that
your sex designation has been changed. You should file this Proposed Order with your
Petition.
As of March 1, 2025, this order may not compel the federal government to respect your
appropriate gender marker or reflect the change in your gender marker on federally
issued documents.
Request for Judicial Intervention Information (“RJI”)
Filing a Request for Judicial Intervention (RJI) form assigns a Judge to your case. For a
name change and/or sex designation change, there is no cost to file an RJI form. After
filing the RJI form with the County Clerk, you will get a receipt for the RJI.
You should file this with your Order and Petition.
Fee Waiver Information
A fee waiver waives the $210 cost associated with opening a case. In order to waive the
filing fees, you will need to fill out and file a Poor Person Order and an Affidavit in Support
of Application to Proceed as a Poor Person when you file your petition.
You must sign (with your current legal name) and date the Affidavit in Support of
Application to Proceed as a Poor Person in front of a notary public.
9
In addition to submitting these documents at the Chief Clerk’s Office along with your
other necessary documents (as is discussed below), you will also need to send a copy of
each set of papers to the County Attorney to:
Erie County Attorney
95 Franklin Street, Room 1634
Buffalo, New York 14202
The County Attorney will review your papers and tell the Court if they agree with your
financial hardship. If the County Attorney agrees with your financial hardship, you will
receive a letter from the Judge stating that your fees have been waived. If the Judge
waives your fees, you do not have to pay the $210 filing fee. If the Judge denies your
request, you must pay the filing fee of $210 within 120 days after the date that the Name
Change Order is signed.
Record Sealing
Effective December 2, 2024, all name change applications shall be immediately sealed
upon filing. The court will take all necessary steps to seal and safeguard the applicant’s:
• Current Name;
• Proposed New Name;
• Current Sex Designation;
• Address (Business and Residential);
• Telephone Numbers;
Or any other personal information to prevent disclosure or unauthorized use. No other
party is allowed access to the application except by an Order of the Court.
Where to Go
After you have filled out the necessary forms, as well as gathered the additional necessary
items and filing fee, you will need to take these documents to the Erie County Clerk’s
Office, Actions & Proceedings Department, at 92 Franklin Street, Buffalo, NY. You should
get an index number for your case. After you receive an index number, go to the Chief
Clerk’s Office at 25 Delaware Avenue, Ground Floor, Buffalo, NY. There, submit a time-
stamped copy of the RJI and all of your original completed documents. If you do not want
to (or cannot) file your documents in person, you can file your papers over the internet
(e-filing).

Gender Marker Change
Each specific documentation listed in the “Updating your Documentation” section of the
guide will provide in-depth instructions about what is necessary for changing your gender
marker on that identification document.
New York State-Issued Documents
No additional documentation is required to change your gender marker on identity
documents issued by New York State. New York now operates on a “self-attestation”
standard regarding gender markers on State-issued documents. This means that you can
check a box at the Department of Health or the Department of Motor Vehicles to self-
select a gender marker. The “self-attestation” standard is effective beginning June 24,
2022.
Federal Documents
As of March 1, 2025, the federal government is not allowing gender marker or “sex
designation” changes to any federally issued documents, even if a New York court has
issued an order recognizing a legal gender designation change.
11
Documents Issued by Other States
Documents issued by other states may require specific supporting documentation, such
as a letter from a doctor or an Order Granting Sex Designation Change. More information
about what a state may require can be found here: https://transequality.org/documents.
Updating Specific Documents
This portion of the guide will include information about how to update various identity
documentation after you have received your Name Change Order from the court.
U.S. Passport
As of March 1, 2025, some people may not want to attempt to change their gender
marker on their passport because the federal government has indicated that it will not
process any gender marker changes on passports.
To update your passport, you will need to submit the following documents to a local
passport agency or post office:
• Evidence of U.S. citizenship. This can be:
o Fully-valid, undamaged U.S. passport (may be expired)
o U.S. birth certificate that meets the following requirements:
 Issued by the city, county, or state of birth
 Lists your full name, date of birth, and place of birth
 Lists your parent(s)' full names
 Has the date filed with registrar's office (must be within one year of
birth)
 Has the registrar's signature
 Has the seal of the issuing authority
o Consular Report of Birth Abroad or Certification of Birth
o Certificate of Naturalization
o Certificate of Citizenship
• Photocopy of evidence of U.S. citizenship (front and back, if there is printed
information on both sides). Photocopies must be:
o Legible
o On white 8.5 inch x 11 inch standard paper
o Black and white
o Single sided
• Photo ID that “resembles your current appearance.” Acceptable forms of ID are:
12
o Valid or expired, undamaged U.S. passport book or passport card
o In-state, fully valid driver's license or enhanced driver's license with photo
o Certificate of Naturalization
o Certificate of Citizenship
o Government employee ID (city, county, state, or federal)
o U.S. military or military dependent ID
o Current (valid) foreign passport
o Matricula Consular (Mexican Consular ID) - commonly used by a parent of a
U.S. citizen child applicant
o U.S. Permanent Resident Card (Green Card) - commonly used by a parent of
a U.S. citizen child applicant
o Trusted Traveler IDs (including valid Global Entry, FAST, SENTRI, and NEXUS
cards)
o Enhanced Tribal Cards and Native American Tribal Photo IDs;
o Other documents: In-state, fully valid learner's permit with photo; in-state,
fully valid non-driver ID with photo; and temporary driver's license with
photo. Note: you may be asked to present an additional ID when presenting
one of these 3 documents.
o If you present an out-of-state ID, you must present an additional ID.
• Photocopy of photo ID (front and back, if there is printed information on both
sides). Photocopies must be:
o Legible
o On white 8.5-inch x 11-inch standard paper
o Black and white
o Single sided
• A 2-inch x 2-inch passport photo that “resembles your current appearance” (these
can be taken at some post offices, the Niagara County Clerk’s Office, and
pharmacies like Walgreens)
• Original or Certified Copy of Name Change Court Order
• Your social security card
Fees: $110 for an updated passport book or $30 for an updated passport card (this fee
may change)
For standard processing method of 10-12 weeks, there are no additional fees. For an
expedited processing method 4-6 weeks, there is an additional fee of $60. There is no
cost for standard delivery of your updated passport book and/or card. For faster delivery,
there is a fee of $17.13. Your supporting documents (such as previous passports/birth
certificates) will be returned to you via USPS first class mail.

U.S. Social Security Card
In order to update your social security card, you will need to do this with the Social
Security Administration. You may update your with the Social Security Administration
either in person or by mail. Many people have their social security cards updated first.
There is no cost to update your name on your social security card. As of March 1, 2025,
the Social Security Administration is not accepting applications for gender marker or “sex
designation” changes.
The following documents are necessary to change your name with the Social Security
Administration:
Form SS-5 (On this form, be sure to use your corrected name. This is the information that
will replace the previous information.)
Certified copy of Order Granting Name Change
Original Birth Certificate
Current (not expired) U.S. driver’s license, U.S. passport, or state-issued non-driver ID
card - If you do not have one of these documents to prove identity and you cannot get a
replacement within 10 days, you can also supply another current photograph
identification document that shows your name, identifying information, a picture of
yourself, and your age or date of birth.
• Current social security card
• Proof of U.S. Citizenship (if you are a U.S. citizen) – this can be one of the
following:
o U.S. Birth Certificate
o U.S. Passport
o Certificate of Naturalization
o Certificate of Citizenship
o Certificate of Report of Birth
o Consular Report of Birth Abroad
• Proof of Immigration (if you are not a U.S. citizen) – this can be one of the
following:
o Form I-551 (Lawful Permanent Resident Card, Machine Readable Immigrant
Visa) with your unexpired foreign passport;
o I-766 (Employment Authorization Document, EAD, work permit); or
o I-94 (Arrival/Departure Record) or admission stamp in the unexpired foreign
passport.
o If you’re an F-1 or M-1 student, you will also need to provide your I-20
(Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status).
o If you’re a J-1 or J-2 exchange visitor, you will also need to provide your DS-
2019 (Certificate of Eligibility for Exchange Visitor Status).
14
The documentation must all be originals or certified copies from the issuing agency. The
social security office will not accept photocopies, notarized copies of documents, or
receipts showing you applied for the document.
New York Driver’s License, Driver’s Permit, or Non-Driver ID
In order to update your driver’s license, learner permit, commercial driver license, or non-
drive ID card, you will need to go to the DMV. There is a fee of $12.50 to update your
driver’s license or learner permit and a fee of $5 to update your non-driver ID card.
The following documents are necessary to change your name on your driver’s license,
permit, or non-driver ID card:
A completed Application for Permit, Driver’s License, or Non-Driver ID Card (MV-44) (Be
sure to fill out this form with your corrected name and gender marker) .
• Your current license, permit, or non-driver ID card, or at least 6 points of proof of
identity and proof of date of birth
o Visit the DMV website to determine what forms of ID are necessary to fulfill
the 6 points of proof of identity and date of birth
• Original or certified copy of the Order Granting Name Change and/or Sex
Designation Change
If you are changing your gender marker, you will need to indicate your corrected gender
marker when filling out the Application for Permit, Driver’s License, or Non-Driver ID Card.
You will not be required to provide any supporting documentation to change your gender
marker.
When changing your name and/or gender marker on a driver’s license, permit, or non-
driver ID at the DMV, you will be required to take a new ID photo. You can also update
your voter registration records at the same time you submit a name change with the
DMV.
Vehicle Registration and Title
If you update your driver’s license, learner’s permit, or non-driver ID, you must also
update your vehicle registration and title. There is no fee to update your registration
documents and title certificate with your corrected name.
The following documents are necessary to change your name on your vehicle registration
and title:
• Your current title or other acceptable proof of ownership
• A completed Vehicle Registration/Title Application (MV-82)
• Your current license, permit, or non-driver ID
15
• Original or certified copy of your Order Granting Name Change and/or Sex
Designation Change
Appealing a Denial of Updated Driver’s License, Permit, or Non-
Driver ID Card
If you are denied an updated driver’s license, permit, or non-driver ID card, you can
appeal the denial. This is done by submitting a completed Administrative Appeal Form
(AA-33A)
by mail to:
DMV Appeals Board
P.O. Box 2935
Albany, NY 12220-0935
Along with the Administrative Appeal Form, you must send a $10 Appeal Fee. This fee

The Administrative Appeal Form and $10 Appeal Fee must be postmarked no more than
sixty (60) days after the date of your denial.

School Records and Diplomas
In most cases, submitting a copy of a name change order to a school secretary or
Registrar is adequate for updating your name in school records. However, you should
contact the institution to ask what exact documentation is required. You may also want to
ask about how to obtain an updated copy of your high school or college diploma.
Voter Registration
If you change your name with the DMV, you can update your voter registration records at
the same time. You can also update your voter registration records online.
To you update your name on your New York voter record directly with County Board of
Elections, you can submit a New York State Voter Registration Form by mail or in person
to the address of the appropriate county board of elections listed on the form.
If you need to check if you are registered to vote, or are looking for information regarding
your polling place or early voting, visit: https://www.nass.org/can-I-vote
New York State Common Benefit Identification Card
If you have a New York State Common Benefit Identification Card (also referred to as an
EBT card), you will want to update your information by calling the customer service
helpline at 1-888-328-6399. A Common Benefit Identification Card is used to identify the
holder as a beneficiary for state benefits, such as SNAP, Medicaid, and cash benefits.
17
New York State Department of Health Benefits
If you receive benefits from the New York State Department of Health (such as Medicaid),
you will need to update with them by calling 1-855-355-5777 or by logging into your web
login and uploading the appropriate documents.
