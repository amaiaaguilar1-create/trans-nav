Once an item has been amended on a vital record, in accordance with evidence requirements, it cannot be changed again unless the State
Registrar receives a court order by a court of competent jurisdiction. The only exception is a change in a cause of death on a death record.
Court-Ordered or Court-Approved Legal Name Change
The person whose name is on the
birth, marriage, or death record needs
to change their legal name on the
record.
Required documents
--Certified court order for the legal name change to correct a RI birth
certificate.
--An amended birth certificate to correct a marriage or death certificate.
--Government-issued photo ID:
• Driver’s license
• Passport
• Military ID
• Certificate of Naturalization
• Alien Registration Card
--Rhode Island residents:
• A Probate Court order from the probate court in the city or town
where you live
• A Family Court order is acceptable for children younger than 18.
Call Family Court at 401-458-3100 for information.
--Out-of-state residents:
• Contact your family court for help.
Fees
• $10 to make the
change on the record
For a copy of the changed
record:
• $22 for in-person
customers
• $25 via mail
Change the Sex Field on a Rhode Island Birth Certificate
As of October 1, 2019, the State of
Rhode Island has amended the sex
field on birth certificates, which allows
people to make a change to reflect their
sex or gender identity on their birth
certificate with proper documentation.
With proper documentation, people can
change their sex from M to F or F to M
using the affidavit. For people born in
Rhode Island who identify as non-binary
or neither male nor female can change
the sex field to X. People of any age
can make this change; however, minors
need parental or guardian consent to do
so.
Required documents
--The person who wants to change their birth certificate must come to the
Office of Vital Records to self-attest, so staff can create an affidavit that
must be signed by the person whose birth certificate will change. If that
person is younger than 18, their parent, legal guardian, or legal
representative must sign the affidavit.
--Government-issued photo ID:
• Driver’s license
• Passport
• Military ID
• Certificate of Naturalization
• Alien Registration Card
Fees
• $10 to make the
change on the birth
record
For a copy of the changed
record:
• $22 for in-person
customers
• $25 via mail

Correct clerical errors made by a birthing facility on a birth record for a child younger than 1
Examples of clerical errors can include
misspelling of parent’s name,
misspelled parent’s date or place of
birth, misspelled address
Required document
-- The Office of Vital Records will ask the birthing facility for the Birth
Worksheet that was completed at the time of birth.
Fees
• No fee to correct
clerical errors
For a copy of the changed
record:
• $22 for in-person
customers
• $25 via mail
Minor corrections on a birth record for a child younger than 1; unable to verify using Birth Worksheet from birthing facility
Examples of minor corrections can
include addition of the given name of
the child when the given name field is
blank or updated spelling of parent’s
name, date of birth, or place of birth
Required documents
--To correct a misspelling of a parent’s name or incorrect date of birth,
the parent must provide a certified copy of their own birth certificate.
--If the State Registrar approves the correction, they will create an
Affidavit of Amendment. If the State Registrar decides there is not
satisfactory evidence to approve the correction, they will notify the applicant
that the correction is not approved, and the birth record cannot be changed
unless there is a court order by a court of competent jurisdiction.
--Government-issued photo ID for parent:
• Driver’s license
• Passport
• Military ID
• Certificate of Naturalization
• Alien Registration Card
Fees
• No fee to correct
minor errors
For a copy of the changed
record:
• $22 for in-person
customers
• $25 via mail
Minor corrections on a birth record for a child older than 1
An example of a minor correction is a
misspelling of the child’s name.
Required documents
--At least one document showing the child’s full name, date of birth, and
the parents’ names must be presented to the Office of Vital Records.
The document must be created before the child is 7. Examples of a valid
document include a baptismal certificate, school entrance record, or a
pediatrician’s record.
--If the State Registrar approves the correction, they will create an
Affidavit of Amendment. If the State Registrar decides there is not
satisfactory evidence to approve the correction, they will notify the applicant
that the correction is not approved, and the birth record cannot be changed
unless there is a court order by a court of competent jurisdiction.
--Government-issued photo ID for parent:
• Driver’s license
• Passport
• Military ID
• Certificate of Naturalization
• Alien Registration Card

change on the birth
record
