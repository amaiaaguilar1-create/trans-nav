Background
Some state Medicaid policies explicitly include best-practice, medically necessary health care for
transgender people, whereas other state policies explicitly exclude such care. Still other states have
no explicit policy either way. Importantly, even in states with no explicit policy or mention of
coverage, transgender people may still be able to access gender-affirming coverage or benefits.
However, when states have no explicit policy, transgender people are more likely to report obstacles
to receiving care, including being denied needed care.
Scoring options:
(1) State Medicaid policy explicitly covers transgender-related health care
(0) State has unclear or no explicit policy regarding Medicaid coverage of transgender-related
health care
(-0.5) State Medicaid policy explicitly excludes coverage of transgender-related health care for
minors
(-1) State Medicaid policy explicitly excludes coverage of transgender-related health care for all
ages

State-by-State Sources & More Information
Alabama
• (0) State has unclear or no explicit policy regarding Medicaid coverage of transgender-
related health care
o However, SB184 (2022) bans medical care for transgender minors under age 19
statewide, though makes no explicit mention of the state’s Medicaid program.
Upheld by 11th Circuit Court of Appeals in Boe v. Marshall (July 2024).
Alaska
• (1) State Medicaid policy explicitly covers transgender-related health care
• See “Alaska Department of Health and Social Services regulations re: Medicaid coverage
and payment for gender dysphoria-related services Memorandum” (June 2021).
• Previously, state explicitly excluded coverage of transgender-related health care for all ages
(-1). See 7 AAC 110.405 Physician services coverage and limitations (since 2010).
Overturned by Being v. Crum (June 2021).
Arizona
• (-1) State Medicaid policy explicitly excludes coverage of transgender-related health care
for all ages
• See 9 AAC R9-22-205.B (since at least 2004) excluding “gender reassignment surgeries”
• See also SB1138 (2022) banning medically necessary surgical care for transgender minors,
though it does not ban non-surgical forms of care (e.g., hormone-related medication)
Arkansas
• (-0.5) State Medicaid policy explicitly excludes coverage of transgender-related health care
for minors
• See AR Code § 20-9-1502 (2024) and HB 1570 (2021), banning medical care for transgender
youth, including explicitly banning Medicaid coverage for minors, among other provisions.
• Lawsuit history: Brandt et al v. Rutledge et al (filed 2021), against HB1570. While the ban on
medical care for minors was blocked by court order multiple times, the 8th Circuit Court of
Appeals ultimately upheld the ban following the Supreme Court’s ruling in US v. Skrmetti
(August 2025).
• Previously, state had no explicit policy regarding coverage of transgender-related care.
Policy instituted by HB 1570 (2021).

Florida
• (-1) State Medicaid policy explicitly excludes coverage of transgender-related health care
for all ages
• See Rule 59G-1.050(7), published August 10, 2022, and effective August 21, 2022. See also
S254 (2023; see Section 3) prohibiting this healthcare for minors statewide and prohibiting
the use of any state funds for this health care, regardless of age.
• Lawsuit history:
o Dekker v. Weida (filed Sept 2022; formerly Dekker v. Marstiller), against Rule 59G-
1.050(7). In June 2023, a federal judge blocked the state’s Medicaid ban. However,
the state is appealing the ruling to the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals, which is still
pending, with clerk to check status September 2026.
o Doe v. Ladapo (filed 2023), against S254’s ban on transgender-related care for
minors). While lower courts blocked the law, the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals
reversed those blocks, meaning the law is currently in effect.
• Formerly (prior to 2022 rule), state Medicaid had no explicit policy regarding coverage of
transgender-related health care (0).
Georgia
• (1) State Medicaid policy explicitly covers transgender-related health care
o However, SB140 (2023) bans medical care for transgender minors statewide (i.e.,
not only in Medicaid), though the law makes no explicit mention of the state’s
Medicaid program. See also related lawsuit Koe v. Noggle. Still, this likely means
that Medicaid will not cover care for minors, even while it may cover it for adults.
• Formerly, state explicitly excluded coverage of transgender-related health care for all ages
(-1). See State Plan (p1c; since 1992). Overturned in 2022 by Thomas et al v. Georgia
Department of Community Health, and see more coverage of case outcome here.

Iowa
• (-1) State Medicaid policy explicitly excludes coverage of transgender-related health care
for all ages
• See Iowa Medicaid Informational Letter 2693 MC-FFS (2025), published in response to
Section 14 of HF1049 (2025). See also Iowa Medicaid Informational Letter 2517-MC-FFS
(2023), published in response to SF538 (2023) which prohibits this health care statewide for
minors.
• Previously and/or related:
o Prior to March 2019, the state explicitly excluded transgender-related health care
coverage in Medicaid. See 441 IAC 78.1(249A).
o In March 2019, the Iowa Supreme Court ruled that the state's previous exclusion of
transgender health coverage violated the state's civil rights law and therefore was
illegal. The decision found that this health care is medically necessary and
therefore may not be excluded.
o In May 2019, the governor signed a law (HF766) that allows Medicaid and other
state-funded health care providers to choose not to cover transgender-related care,
though this did not create new statewide exclusions.
o In November 2021, an Iowa District Court ruled that denying Medicaid coverage for
transition-related care (as was permitted by HF766) violates the state’s law and
constitution, and ordering the state’s Department of Human Services to provide
coverage when medically necessary.
▪ In 2022, Iowa DHS issued Policy Clarfication 228 affirming Medicaid
coverage of prescription medication for treatment of gender dysphoria,
indicating ongoing coverage at that time.
o In 2023, the state enacted SF538 (March 2023), banning medical care for
transgender minors statewide (i.e., not only via Medicaid coverage). While the bill
did not explicitly mention the Medicaid program, the state later issued Iowa
Medicaid Informational Letter 2517-MC-FFS (Sept 2023) prohibiting coverage in the
state Medicaid program (-0.5).
o In February 2025, the state removed “gender identity” from the list of protected
characteristics in the state’s civil rights law (via SF418)—undermining the 2019
State Supreme Court ruling’s basis—and later explicitly prohibited Medicaid from
covering transgender-related health care (HF1049 and Iowa Medicaid Informational
Letter 2693 MC-FFS (2025)) regardless of age (-1).
