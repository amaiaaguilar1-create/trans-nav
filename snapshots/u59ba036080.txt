*Allen v. Mearns (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Ind.) [Filed 3/26
These two cases were filed on behalf of a combined 11 undergraduate students at Ball State University, who were disciplined by the university after they entered an administration building with the goal of leaving notes for the university’s president expressing their opposition to the university’s refusal to divest from Israel. They left the building promptly at 5:00pm when it closed. They were disciplined for violating two policies: one prohibiting protests, demonstrations, or assemblies within 50 feet of most buildings on campus, and one requiring students to abide by any directives given to them by university personnel acting within the scope of their duties. Because one of the students was already on “conduct probation,” he received harsher discipline than the other students and was suspended for the spring 2026 semester. We have claimed that the 50-foot policy violates the First Amendment and the other policy violates due process. Because the students all wish to engage in expressive activities near campus buildings in the future, we sought a preliminary injunction against the prospective enforcement of the 50-foot policy. After we filed that motion, Ball Stand changed its policies so that they now prohibit any “protest or demonstration” within campus buildings or within 25 feet of those buildings if they are “audible” from within certain portions of the buildings. We have continued to argue that the now-current policies violate the First Amendment. The cases are proceeding.
ATTORNEYS: Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor, Joshua T. Bleish
Brock v. Town of Kouts (U.S. Dist. Ct. – No. Dist. of Ind.) [Filed 9/26]
The Town of Kouts has a sign ordinance that violates the First Amendment as it prohibits political signs on residential property unless it is less than 30 days prior to the election. After the suit was filed, the Town agreed to repeal the ordinance. Once the case is formally settled, it will be closed.

Exodus Immigration Inc. v. Rokita (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Ind., Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals) [Filed 9/25]
Attorney General Todd Rokita served a Civil Investigative Demand (“CID”) on Exodus, demanding responses to 39 interrogatories and 28 separate requests for production of documents seeking, among other things, an overwhelming amount of information as to Exodus’s work with its clients for the last three years, communications concerning U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), and Exodus’s work with persons who are present in the United States without lawful status. The CID is part of a continuing pattern by the Attorney General of sending unreasonable and unlawful requests to immigrant service organizations and other entities across Indiana. These appear to be designed to advance a personal political agenda and to punish and deter organizations that assist, advocate for, and associate with immigrants and refugees. The investigation is without cause and is in bad faith, and we have challenged it on the grounds that it violates the First Amendment. The district court entered a preliminary injunction in plaintiff’s favor and the case is being appealed by the Attorney General’s office.
ATTORNEYS: Kenneth J. Falk, Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor, Joshua T. Bleisch
Lawmaster v. Spivey (U.S. Dist. Ct. - So. Dist. Of Ind.) (Filed 6/26)
Lee Lawmaster made comments to a number of elected officials Facebook pages that read only “86-[name of official].” He was visited by Kurt Spivey who is the Director of Investigations for the Indiana Attorney General’s office. Mr. Spivey, in a conversation that was filmed by a security camera, indicated that his posting of “86” was a threat and that Mr. Lawmaster had “crossed the line” and “we could easily indict you over this today.” Mr. Spivey cautioned Mr. Lawmaster "to tone it down a little bit.” We are seeking both an injunction and damages. We have fully briefed a motion for preliminary injunction and defendant has fully briefed a motion to dismiss.
ATTORNEYS: Joshua T. Bleisch, Kenneth J. Falk, Stevie J. Pactor, Gavin M. Rose
Luken v. Snyder (U.S. Dist. Ct. – No. Dist. of Ind.) [filed 7/25]
Ms. Luken was a teacher who volunteered to be a chaperone on a school trip to Washington, D.C. that was organized by a private company, not her employe school district. While on the trip she posed with a friend in front of the White House with a t-shirt reading “8647.” She posted it briefly on her Facebook page. After the post received negative attention she was ultimately told she had to resign as a teacher or be fired. She resigned. The case claims a violation of the First Amendment. Cross-motions for summary judgment are being briefed
ATTORNEYS: Joshua T. Bleisch, Kenneth J. Falk
Patoka Valley AIDS Community Action Group, Inc. v. City of Loogootee (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Ind., Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals) [Filed 6/24], and new case filed 1/26
This challenged the denial of the ability of plaintiff to conduct a Pride festival in Loogootee and the constitutionality of a hastily drawn up ordinance to regulate a public events ordinance. Although the festival was eventually allowed to occur in 2024, it was denied again in 2025, and Loogootee passed several iterations of new, but constitutionally infirm, ordinances. We sought another preliminary injunction and summary judgment simultaneously. The court granted our motion for summary judgment as to liability, and we settled the claim regarding the client’s damages. The defendant did not appeal, and the case was closed.
However, in a new turn of events, the City passed yet another new ordinance which reenacted portions of the old ordinance that the Court specifically found to violate the Constitution. We filed a motion in the closed case to hold the City in contempt, We also filed a new case challenging that ordinance. Following our contempt motion and the filing of the new case, the City again amended the ordinance, but several of its unconstitutional provision still remain. The Court held a hearing in March and found the City in contempt and ordered it to change its policy. We are asking for a preliminary injunction so that the 2026 Pride festival may occur as planned in September, and have asked that the Court consolidate the preliminary injunction with a decision on the merits. The Court ruled in plaintiff’s favor and entered a permanent injunction and Loogootee is appealing to the Seventh Circuit.
ATTORNEYS: Kenneth J. Falk, Stevie J. Pactor, Joshua T. Bleisch
Pitcher v. Wayne County, Indiana (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Indiana) [Filed 2/26]
The plaintiff is a current employee of the Wayne County Coroner’s Office. His personal Facebook account was “blocked” from the official page of the Wayne County Commissioners, apparently due to personal grievances. We filed suit alleging that this violates the First Amendment. The case has since been resolved and will be closed.
ATTORNEYS: Gavin M. Rose, Joshua T. Bleisch
Ruiz v. City of Indianapolis (U.S. Dist. Co. - So. Dist. of Ind.) [Filed 4/26]
Mianna Ruiz and Elaine Thiel are two longtime volunteers at the shelter operated by Indianapolis Animal Care Services (“IACS”), who we previously represented in an action challenging the constitutionality of certain policies applicable to volunteers. In November 2025, IACS disseminated a new Volunteer Handbook applicable to its volunteers. As is relevant here, that handbook prohibits volunteers (a) from engaging in any “disrespectful” behavior (including on social media) and (b) from disseminating any information that they learn as volunteers if that information is not “readily available to the public.” We have filed a lawsuit alleging that both provisions violate the First Amendment. The case is proceeding although the parties have entered settlement negotiations.
ATTORNEYS: Gavin M. Rose
Smiley v. Jenner (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Indiana; 7th Circuit Court of Appeals) [Filed 6/23]
This is a challenge to a portion of House Enrolled Act 1608, effective July 1, 2023, that prohibits schools and teachers from engaging in “instruction” on “human sexuality” to students who are pre-K through third grade. Our lawsuit, brought on behalf of a teacher, argues that the law is both unconstitutionally vague and violative of the First Amendment. The Court denied our request for a preliminary injunction and the matter is being appealed to the 7th Circuit and was argued in February of 2024. The Court recently ruled against us, and the matter is being closed.
ATTORNEYS: Kenneth J. Falk, Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor
Sweeney v. Commissioner, Indiana Department of Correction (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Indiana; U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit [Filed 10/17]
This case challenges a new policy of the DOC that requires that all incoming non-legal correspondence be in plain white envelopes and requires that the correspondence “be on originally purchased, plain white, lined paper (no photocopies).” The case alleges that this violates the First Amendment. The Court certified the case as a class action and entered a preliminary injunction for the plaintiffs. The DOC dismissed its appeal of the preliminary injunction, and the parties reached a settlement. The district court found that the settlement was fair, reasonable, and adequate as required of class actions. The case remains open for monitoring.
ATTORNEY: Kenneth J. Falk
Wirtshafter v. Trustees of Indiana University (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Ind.) [Filed 5/24] + Akou v. Trustees of Indiana University (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. of Ind.) [Filed 8/24]
These consolidated cases challenge (1) the constitutionality of the no-trespass orders issued to the plaintiffs following their participating in demonstration on Dunn Meadow on the IU Bloomington campus in April that prohibited the plaintiff from returning to campus to exercise their First amendment rights; and (2) Indiana University’s expressive activity policy that prohibits all expressive activities after 11:00 pm. and before 6:00 a.m. IU amended the challenged policy, but the new policy remains unconstitutional and a preliminary injunction was entered in our favor. IU then amended the challenged policy once again. The Court recently issued its decision on cross-motions for summary judgment, which was a mixed but positive result for our clients. We won on our claim that the professors’ discipline violated the First Amendment and the Court ordered it expunged from their personnel records. The Court also held that the Professors’ First Amendment rights were violated when they were given the no-trespass orders but that the defendants were entitled to qualified immunity on the basis that there was no clearly established law on point at the time of the violation. No appeals were filed. We have sought attorneys’ fees and the University has also sought fees and costs. The matter has been briefed and we are awaiting a decision on the fee and cost claims.
ATTORNEYS: Kenneth J. Falk, Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor

A.C. v. Metropolitan School District of Martinsville et al (U.S. Dist. Ct. – So. Dist. Of Ind., Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals; U.S. Supreme Court) [12/21]
The plaintiff is a transgender boy who, when we filed the case, was attending a middle school within the Metropolitan School District of Martinsville. He has been diagnosed with and receives treatment for gender dysphoria. He wishes to use male facilities in the school He has been required to use either the girls’ restrooms or the single-person restroom in the nurse’s office. The district court granted a preliminary injunction and the school district has appealed to the Seventh Circuit. This appeal has been consolidated with the appeal of the B.E. case. The Seventh Circuit issued a ruling for us in August and the school filed for certiorari in the U.S. Supreme Court, which the Supreme Court denied in January. The matter has now been sent back to the district court for further proceedings. The plaintiff is now a student at Martinsville High School. It is set for trial on March. The district court granted plaintiff a permanent injunction concerning restroom usage. Martinsville has appealed the permanent injunction and the matter is fully briefed. The Seventh Circuit held off holding argument pending a decision in the B.P.J. case. Now that the Supreme Court decided B.P.J. , the Seventh Circuit heard oral argument on September 28, 2026 and we are awaiting a decision.

K.C. v. Individual Members of the Medical Licensing Board (U.S. Dist. Ct.—So. Dist. of Ind; Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals) [Filed 4/23]
This is the challenge to the transgender health ban (S.E.A. 480). The district court has entered a preliminary injunction preventing the law from going into effect. The State has appealed the decision and the case was argued in February in the 7th Circuit. Although the panel has not yet issued a decision, shortly after the argument it issued a stay of the district court’s injunction, putting the law back into effect. The State had not asked for this stay; the panel did it on its own, which is extremely unusual. This means the law is in effect and gender-affirming hormones can no longer be provided to minors. Additionally, the aiding and abetting provision of the law, also back in effect, prohibits doctors and other practitioners from referring their patients for care out-of-state or even discussing their former patients with out-of-state practitioner seeking to assist the youth. The Seventh Circuit ruled against plaintiffs in a 2-1 decision. The Seventh Circuit denied further review. Skrmetti has now been decided, and the case was sent back to the district court. We have negotiated a dismissal of the remaining claims. There are multiple classes involved in the case and, before it can be dismissed, notice must be given and the district court must conduct a fairness hearing, which his set for September 10. The Court approved the dismissal and the case will be closed.
ATTORNEYS: Kenneth J. Falk, Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor. From National ACLU: Chase Strangio, Harper Seldin
Pride Center of Terre Haute, Inc. v. President, Indiana State University (“Work Study case”) (U.S. Dist.. Ct. - So. Dist. of Ind., Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals) [Filed 9/25]
The Pride Center of Terre Haute, Inc. (“Pride Center”) is a non-profit organization that is dedicated to advocating for and creating a positive impact on the lives of LGBTQ+ individuals in and around Vigo County. However, it serves and assists all persons, regardless of their gender identity or sexual orientation. It is operated almost entirely by volunteers, although it has been able to also use student workers from Indiana State University who are paid in part by the Pride Center and in part by federal work-study funds and grants from the Sycamore Community Work Program both of which are administered by Indiana State University. Recently, Indiana State University has terminated all funding for the Pride Center, erroneously claiming that a “Best Practices” statement issued by the United States Department of Justice to avoid discrimination mandates the termination. We have challenged this termination on First Amendment grounds. We sought a preliminary injunction, which was denied and we appealed the case. The Seventh Circuit heard oral argument on October 1, 2026, and we are awaiting a decision.
ATTORNEYS: Kenneth J. Falk, Stevie J. Pactor, Joshua T. Bleisch
In re the Change of Gender of V.E. (Ind. Court of Appeals) [Appeal docketed 2/24/26]
V.E., a transgender woman, filed a petition for a court ordered change of gender marker in November 2024. Her local county trial court granted that petition in March 2025. Nearly four months later, the State of Indiana filed a motion to intervene in V.E.’s gender change petition so it could argue that the court order was contrary to state law. The trial court denied the State’s motion to intervene, and the State appealed. We argue in the Indiana Court of Appeals that the trial court made the right decision when it denied the State’s attempt at intervention in this closed case.
ATTORNEYS: Joshua T. Bleisch, Kenneth J. Falk, Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor
In re the Matter of Z.C. (Marion Co. Superior Court)
R.C. is a transgender woman who filed a petition for a change of name and gender marker in August 2025. In October 2025, the trial court granted R.C.’s petition. The Seventh Circuit ruled against plaintiffs in a 2-1 decision. The Seventh Circuit denied further review. Skrmetti has now been decided, and the case was sent back to the district court. We have negotiated a dismissal of the remaining claims. There are multiple classes involved in the case and, before it can be dismissed, notice must be given and the district court must conduct a fairness hearing, which his set for September 10. The Court approved the dismissal and the case will be closed.

Planned Parenthood of Indiana and Kentucky, Inc. v. Commissioner, Indiana State Department of Health - (U.S. Dist. Ct – So. Dist. of Ind., Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals, United States Supreme Court) [Filed 5/17]
This case challenges portions of Senate Enrolled Act No. 404 that amends Indiana law to:
1) Provide that in a bypass proceeding where a minor can seek judicial permission to obtain an abortion without parental consent the court can order that notice be provided to the parent; requires physicians to execute an affidavit where a parent consents to a minor’s abortion after receiving a photo ID and other unspecified evidence that “a reasonable person under similar circumstances would rely on the information provided . . . as sufficient evidence of [the] identity and relationship” between the minor and parent, and (3) prohibit persons from aiding or assisting an unemancipated minor from seeking an abortion from obtaining one without satisfying the consent or bypass requirements under Indiana law. This last provision would prohibit PPINK from advising minors that they could obtain abortions out of state. A preliminary injunction was granted, and the State is appealing the portion of the preliminary injunction prohibiting it from enforcing the parental-notice requirement. The Seventh Circuit affirmed by a 2-1 decision and rehearing en banc was denied. The State sought review in the United States Supreme Court. On July 2, 2020, the Supreme Court granted certiorari and remanded the case back to the Seventh Circuit for reconsideration in light of June Medical Services. The Seventh Circuit again affirmed the District Court and the State again sought review in the U.S. Supreme Court. The Supreme Court reversed in light of Dobbs and sent the case back. Following Dobbs the State moved to vacate the preliminary injunction, and we have indicated that we have no grounds to oppose the motion. Dobbs doomed the first argument concerning the notice issue and changes to Indiana’s abortion laws meant that we could no longer pursue the vagueness claims concerning the identification requirements, but we continued to pursue the First Amendment issue. The district court entered summary judgment in our favor on this issue and enjoined the enforcement of the statute. The State appealed to the Seventh Circuit and the court recently ruled in our favor. The State has sought rehearing en banc and the matter is pending.

Ind. Protection & Advocacy Servs. Comm’n v. Ind. Family & Soc. Servs. Admin. - (U.S. Dist. Ct.—So. Dist. of Indiana) [Filed 5/24]
The Health and Wellness Medicaid Waiver Program is a Medicaid home- and community-based waiver program approved by the federal government. Through this program, eligible persons with disabilities who might otherwise require institutionalization are able to receive services necessary to ensure that they may live in a community environment. For years, the Indiana Family and Social Services Administration, which operates the program, has allowed the parents or other “legally responsible individuals” of waiver enrollees to be reimbursed for providing personal care services to enrollees. This has been absolutely vital for many persons with significant medical needs because home nursing is not universally available and because family members of persons with disabilities have often been trained to attend to medical needs in a way that other potential caretakers have not been. These needs might include G-tube feedings, seizure monitoring and responses, medication administration, and a wide variety of other issues (in addition to, of course, assistance with the activities of daily living). Effective July 1, 2024, FSSA ceased allowing these family members to continue providing these services.
Our clients are the Indiana Protection and Advocacy Services Administration (which has authority under federal law to pursue claims on behalf of persons with disabilities) as well as two medically fragile children with significant medical needs. We have alleged that FSSA’s changes place enrollees at significant risk of institutionalization and therefore violate the “integration mandate” of the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Rehabilitation Act. We have further alleged that FSSA’s failure to ensure the availability of home nursing violates Medicaid law. The district court issued a preliminary injunction in favor of the two individual plaintiffs only. On appeal, the Seventh Circuit affirmed the injunction in our favor. The case has been returned to the district court and the parties have reached a settlement.

Michael Shannon is an inmate at Pendleton Correctional Facility who, after a photograph that had been in his possession and received through the mail was discovered, was disciplined by prison officials for violating a rule prohibiting him from possessing certain “personal information” of other persons. The disciplinary proceedings included a number of procedural anomalies, ranging from officials changing the rule he was alleged to have violated after the hearing to uncertainty over what precisely he was alleged to have done wrong. We filed two cases raising due process challenges to the proceedings. Because of a dispute in authority on a procedural issue—the proper mechanism for challenging prison discipline that resulted in a loss of credit time for an inmate sentenced to life (such that he does not earn credit time)—it was necessary to file two separate actions. We have asked that the actions be consolidated, and that request is pending.

State v. Leach (Huntington Circuit Court)
The client was ordered to pay $50 a week to pay a debt owing to Community Corrections. The order specified that if she failed to do so she would be imprisoned for 60 days. The client was unable to pay as she was pregnant and close to delivery. We filed a motion that sought to relieve her from this obligation. The Court extended the time for her to start paying until July.
ATTORNEY: Kenneth J. Falk
Vasel v. Braun - (Monroe County Superior Court) [Filed May 2025]
Prior to May 6, 2025, every institution of higher learning in Indiana that predominantly offers four-year baccalaureate degrees and offers graduate degrees provided a method for alumni of the institution to select members of the institution’s board of trustees. However, Indiana’s recently enacted budget bill, HEA 1001, signed into law by the Governor on May 6, 2025, contains provisions at Indiana Code § 21-20-3-2, et seq. (eff. May 6, 2025), which repealed prior law that gave Indiana University alumni a voice in selecting members of the board of trustees. There is no formal alumni input anymore, and the Governor has removed the three elected trustees and appointed three new ones. The suit alleges that the new law violates the Indiana Constitution’s prohibition of “local” laws. The Court awarded summary judgment to the Governor and we are not appealing. The matter will be closed.
ATTORNEYS: Kenneth J. Falk, Stevie J. Pactor, Gavin M. Rose
Cupp v. Norwell Community Schools (Wells County Circuit Court) [Filed 2/2026)
Ms. Cupp’s daughter has been subjected to the frequent use of restraint and seclusion in her public school. Ms. Cupp has sought a variety of records regarding the district’s policies as to the use of seclusion and restraint, as well as documents specific to her daughter. Despite the clear command of the Indiana Access to Public Records Act, Indiana Code § 5-14-3 (“APRA”), Norwell Community Schools (“NCS”) refuses to provide the requested public records. We have filed a complaint under APRA seeking those records. Note: an attorney from the National Youth Law Center brought this issue to our attention and is working with us informally. It is anticipated that the documents being withheld will contain evidence of violations of various federal laws pertaining to special education, and if that is so, NYLC will continue to represent the client as appropriate on those issues.
ATTORNEYS: Stevie J. Pactor, Kenneth J. Falk
*Litherland v. State (Court of Appeals of Indiana) [Appeal filed 9/26]
Mr. Litherland pleaded guilty in 2016 to two sex offenses regarding children, in which neither victim was his own child. He has completed his term of incarceration and has been released, subject to a term of probation. One of his probation conditions states that he may not have any contact with anyone under the age of 16 (including his own children, who are 14-year-old twins) without court approval. He filed a pro se petition to modify that probation condition, which the twins’ mother did not oppose, and it was denied. We are representing him in his appeal.
ATTORNEYS: Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor
Mosha v. English (U.S. Dist. Ct. – No. Dist. of Ind.) [filed 4/26]
Yury Mosha is a Russian national who is currently detained at Miami Correctional Facility while his immigration case proceeds. Under federal law, he is not eligible for release on bond while that case proceeds. In conjunction with attorneys from the Legal Aid Society of New York, who represent him in his immigration case and who are serving as lead counsel, we have filed a petition for a writ of habeas corpus challenging the failure to provide him with a meaningful opportunity to be released on bond. He has now been detained for more than a year and a half. The habeas petition has been fully briefed, and we are waiting on a decision.
ATTORNEYS: Gavin M. Rose, Stevie J. Pactor, Legal Aid Society of N.Y.
Rokita v. IPS (Marion County Superior Court)
We’re in the unusual (for us) position of representing the defendant in this case. Attorney General Rokita filed an action against Indianapolis Public Schools alleging that their policies violate Indiana law by failing to ensure cooperation with federal immigration enforcement “to the greatest extent possible.” The trial court denied the Attorney’s General’s motion for a preliminary injunction and the Attorney General is not appealing. We are awaiting the next move.
