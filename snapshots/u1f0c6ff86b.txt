What to File to Change a Minor’s Name
This page is a checklist of documents you will need to file to legally change a minor’s name. See
“How to Fill Out a Name Change Request for a Minor” for step-by-step instructions for the numbered
parts of the Request. Read all instructions before you fill out any forms.
1. Complete and bring the following documents to the Clerk’s Office:
□ Non-Criminal Case Cover Sheet – File one original and at least one copy. Fill out only the top
left box under “Petitioner.” The rest of the form is complete. The Petitioner is the adult filling out
the form. After the Petitioner’s name, write “on behalf of,” then the minor’s name.
□ Minor Name Change Request (Petition) – File one original and at least one copy.
□ Order to Show Cause (OSC) – File four originals and at least one copy. Fill out sections one
and two.
□ Application to Waive Court Costs and Fees (Optional) – Fill this out if you cannot pay the fees
for this case. If you choose to file this, file one original and at least one copy.
2. Post the Order to Show Cause:
□ Once the court has reviewed your filings, the Clerk will send you signed copies of the OSC.
These must be posted in public places by someone other than you. The minor’s parents
cannot post the OSC. The OSC must be posted in three public places in Hagåtña and one
public place in the village where the minor lives for four weeks. Places to post the OSC
include the post office, court, mayor’s office, and stores or other public businesses.
3. After posting, return to the Clerk’s Office with these documents:
□ Declaration of Posting – File one original and at least one copy.
□ Final Order – File one original and at least one copy. Fill out sections one and two.
There is no fee to file the Declaration of Posting or Final Order.

How to Fill Out a Name Change Request for a Minor
What is a Name Change Request for a Minor?
→ This form asks the court to legally change the name of a minor under 18 years old. Once
approved by the court, the minor’s new name becomes their official legal name and can be used
on all documents, including their driver’s license, passport, and Social Security card.
→ You might use this form if:
• You want to change the first, middle, or last name of a minor
• You are the parent or guardian of a minor who wants to use a name that better reflects their
identity
• You adopted a child and want to change their last name
• Another reason
Can you use this form?
YES, if:
1. You are 18 years or older AND
2. You are the parent of the minor whose name you want to change
→ OR: if both the minor’s parents are deceased, you may file if you are the minor’s legal
guardian
NO, if:
• You are neither the minor’s parent nor guardian OR
• You are the minor’s legal guardian, but one or both of the minor’s parents are still living.

Before you start
• Read these instructions and the whole form before responding to any questions.
• Be honest. Lying to the court is against the law.
• Fill in every question. If you need more space, include “continued on additional page” next to
the question on the form. On the additional page, include the question number and finish your
response. Include your name on each additional page. Attach the additional pages to your
request.
What you should have before filing
1. The minor’s birth certificate.
2. If the minor’s current name is different than what is recorded on the birth certificate, then you
will need a government-issued photo ID (driver’s license, passport, etc.) with the minor’s
current name OR a court order with the minor’s current name.
3. Proof of your residency (for example, mayor’s verification or Guam ID).
4. A copy of your government-issued photo ID to prove your identity and relationship to the
minor.
5. If you are the minor’s legal guardian, be prepared to show the court the death certificates of
both of the minor’s parents. A legal guardian can only file a name change petition on behalf of
a minor if both of the minor’s parents have died. The court may also ask you to file, under seal,
your proof of guardianship. The Clerk’s Office can help you with this process if the court
requests a sealed (confidential) filing.
If you do not have one or more of these documents, you may still file a request to change the
minor’s name and explain to the court why you do not have them. Use the space provided or
attach an additional page with your explanation and file it with your Request.

Step 1: Tell the court how to reach you
Fill in your name, mailing and physical address (if different), and contact information.
Step 2: Language help
Need an interpreter? Check the box and write the language you speak. If you need language
assistance to fill out this form, contact the court. Court navigators can help you.
Step 3: About the minor
Fill in the minor’s current legal name, birth date, and birthplace.
Step 4: Write exactly the name the minor wants
Write the name exactly as you want it to appear on the minor’s new documents.
Step 5: Tell the court why you want to change the minor’s name
Explain your reason for changing the minor’s name. Be honest and specific. Include the reasons
the minor asked you to make this request. If the minor has changed their name before, list each
previous name and explain why it was changed.
Step 6: About the minor’s other parent or parents
Tell the court who the minor’s other parent is. If you don’t know, you can write “unknown.” If the
other parent is deceased, write the parent’s name followed by “deceased.”
Step 7: Sign your form
Fill in today’s date, sign your name, and print your name clearly. By signing, you are promising the
court that everything you wrote is true.
Important reminders
• Be honest. Lying to the court is against the law.
• Keep copies of everything you file.
• After the court approves the minor’s name change, you must update their name with
government agencies yourself. The court does not do this for you.

What happens next
1. File your form at the Superior Court of Guam Clerk’s Office. Bring the originals of each of your
documents, and bring four copies of the Order to Show Cause (OSC).
→ Superior Court of Guam
120 West O’Brien Dr.
Hagåtña, GU 96910
671-475-3331
→ Northern Court Satellite
132 West Marine Corps Dr.
Dededo, GU 96929
671-635-2501
2. Pay the filing fee
→ Ask the Clerk how much the fee is and if you qualify for a fee waiver.
→ File a Request to Waive Court Fees and Costs if you want to ask the court to waive fees.
3. If the court issues an OSC, you must have someone put it somewhere the public can see it.
This is called “posting.” Someone other than you must post the OSC in three public places in
Hagåtña and one public place in the village where the minor lives. Neither of the minor’s
parents can post the OSC. Leave it posted for four weeks. They can post it at the post office,
court, mayor’s office, and stores or other public businesses.
→ The court will want the consent of both the minor’s parents before approving a name
change. If you haven’t already, tell the other parent about the Request. Both parents should
attend the hearing. Tell the other parent once you know when the hearing will be held.
→ Any adult other than the minor’s parents can post the OSC, even a friend or family member.
Process servers are also available for hire. The Clerk’s Office can give you the “Special
Process Server List,” available at guamcourts.gov/quick-assist/specifically/process-servers.
4. After posting for four weeks, return to the court and file the Declaration of Posting and Final
Order. There is no filing fee for either of these forms.
5. The Clerk will send you a hearing date by mail/email.
6. Go to your hearing. Bring proof of the minor’s identification, including their birth certificate.
7. The judge will ask you questions about why you want to change the minor’s name. Answer
honestly. Depending on the minor’s age, the minor may also be asked about the name change.
8. If the judge approves the name change request, you will receive a court order with the minor’s
new legal name. This document is very important—keep it safe! You will need it to update the
minor’s name with the Social Security Administration, DMV, passport office, banks, and other
agencies. The court does not do this for you.

How to Apply to Waive Court Fees and Costs
Use this form if you want to file or respond to a case in either the Supreme Court or the Superior
Court, or have the court serve documents in your case, but you cannot afford to pay the filing fees or
costs. You must attach the completed forms that you want the court to waive fees and costs for.
What is an Application to Waive Court Costs and Fees?
If you cannot pay, this form helps you ask the court to waive costs and fees for:
✓ The filing fee for this case,
✓ Serving (notifying) the other party,
✓ Certification fees,
✓ Making copies of documents, and
✓ Transcripts for appeal.
But the court cannot waive the costs of publication in the newspaper or elsewhere.
What you need
1. Your case number, if you already have one;
2. Information about your income, property, and monthly expenses; and
3. Proof of your income, such as a pay stub, benefits letter, or bank statement.
Before you start
• Read these instructions and the whole form before responding to any questions.
• Be honest. Lying to the court is against the law.
• Fill in every question. If you need more space, include “continued on additional page” next to the
question on the form. On the additional page, include the question number and finish your
response. Include your name and case number on each additional page. Attach the additional
pages to your request.
This page contains instructions only—do not file.
