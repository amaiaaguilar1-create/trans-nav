What to File to Change Your Name (Adults)
This page is a checklist of documents you will need to file to legally change your name. See “How to
Fill Out Your Adult Name Change Request” for step-by-step instructions for the numbered parts of the
Request. Read all instructions before you fill out any forms.
1. Complete and bring the following documents to the Clerk’s Office:
□ Non-Criminal Case Cover Sheet – File one original and at least one copy. Fill out only the top
left box under “Petitioner.” The rest of the form is complete.
□ Adult Name Change Request (Petition) – File one original and at least one copy.
□ Order to Show Cause (OSC) – File four originals and at least one copy. Fill out sections one
and two.
□ Application to Waive Court Costs and Fees (Optional) – Fill this out if you cannot pay the fees
for this case. If you choose to file this, file one original and at least one copy.
2. Post the Order to Show Cause:
□ If the court issues an OSC, the clerk will send it to you via mail/email. After the court signs it,
someone other than you must put it up somewhere the public can see it. It must be posted in
three public places in Hagåtña and one public place in the village where you live. Leave it
posted for four weeks. Places it can be posted include the post office, court, mayor’s office,
and stores or other public businesses.
3. After posting, bring these documents to the Clerk’s Office:
□ Declaration of Posting – File one original and at least one copy.
□ Final Order – File one original and at least one copy. Fill out sections one and two.
There is no fee to file the Declaration of Posting or Final Order.

How to Fill Out Your Adult Name Change Request
What is an Adult Name Change Request?
→ This form asks the court to legally change your name. You must be 18 or older to request a
name change as an adult. Once approved by the court, your new name becomes your official
legal name and can be used on all documents, such as your driver’s license, passport, and
Social Security card.
→ You might use this form if:
• You got married or divorced
• You want to change your first, middle, or last name
• You want to use a name that better reflects your identity
• Another personal reason.
Before you start
• Read these instructions and the whole form before responding to any questions.
• Be honest. Lying to the court is against the law.
• Fill in every question. If you need more space, include “continued on additional page” next to
the question on the form. On the additional page, include the question number and finish your
response. Include your name on each additional page. Attach the additional pages to your
request.
What you should have before filing
1. Birth certificate,
2. One government-issued photo ID (for example, a license or passport), and
3. Proof of residency (for example, mayor's verification, utility bill, or lease).
If you do not have one or more of these documents, you may still file your request to change your
name and explain to the court why you do not have them.

Step 1: About you
Fill in your current legal name, mailing and physical address (if different), and contact information.
Include your birth date and birthplace.
Step 2: Language help
Need an interpreter? Check the box and fill in the language you speak. If you need language
assistance to fill out this form, contact the court. Court navigators can help you.
Step 3: About your mother and father
Fill in information about at least one of your parents. The court uses this to verify your identity. If
you don’t have this information or don’t know it, include a relative’s information.
Step 4: Write exactly the name you want
Write the name exactly as you want it to appear on your new documents.
Step 5: Tell the court why you want to change your name
Explain your reason for changing your name. Be honest and specific. If you have changed your
name or gone by another name before, list each previous name and explain why you changed it.
Step 6: If applicable, give more information
For each question, answer Yes or No. If your answer to any question is Yes, then explain your
situation and give the judge as much information as they need to make a decision.
Step 7: Sign your form
Fill in today’s date, sign your name, and print your name clearly. By signing, you are promising the
court that everything you wrote is true.
Important reminders
• Keep copies of everything you file.
• Changing your name does not erase debts, warrants, criminal records, or other legal
obligations.
• After the court approves your name change, you must update your name with government
agencies and creditors yourself. The court does not do this for you.

What happens next
1. File your form at the Superior Court of Guam Clerk’s Office. Bring the originals of each of your
documents and bring four copies of the order to show cause (OSC).
→ Superior Court of Guam

671-475-3331
→ Northern Court Satellite
132 West Marine Corps Dr.
Dededo, GU 96929
671-635-2501
2. Pay the filing fee
→ Ask the Clerk how much the fee is and if you qualify for a fee waiver.
→ File an Application to Waive Court Costs and Fees if you cannot pay.
3. If the court issues an OSC, you must have someone put it somewhere the public can see it.
This is called “posting.” Someone other than you must post the OSC in three public places in
Hagåtña and one public place in the village where you live. Leave it posted for four weeks.
They can post it at the post office, court, mayor’s office, and stores or other public businesses.
→ Any adult other than you can post the OSC, even a friend or family member. Process
servers are also available for hire. The Clerk’s Office can give you the “Special Process
Server List,” also available at guamcourts.gov/quick-assist/specifically/process-servers.
4. After posting for four weeks, return to the Clerk’s Office and file the Declaration of Posting and
Final Order. There is no filing fee for either of these forms.
5. The Clerk will send you a hearing date by mail/email.
6. Go to your hearing. Bring proof of identification, including your birth certificate, government-
issued photo ID, and proof of residency.
7. The judge will ask you questions about why you want to change your name. Answer honestly.
8. If the judge approves your name change request, you will receive a court order with your new
legal name. This document is very important—keep it safe! You will need it to update your name
with the Social Security Administration, the DMV, voter registration, the passport office, banks,
and other agencies. The court does not do this for you.

How to Apply to Waive Court Fees and Costs
Use this form if you want to file or respond to a case in either the Supreme Court or the Superior
Court, or have the court serve documents in your case, but you cannot afford to pay the filing fees or
costs. You must attach the completed forms that you want the court to waive fees and costs for.
What is an Application to Waive Court Costs and Fees?
If you cannot pay, this form helps you ask the court to waive costs and fees for:
✓ The filing fee for this case,
✓ Serving (notifying) the other party,
✓ Certification fees,
✓ Making copies of documents, and
✓ Transcripts for appeal.
But the court cannot waive the costs of publication in the newspaper or elsewhere.
What you need
1. Your case number, if you already have one;
2. Information about your income, property, and monthly expenses; and
3. Proof of your income, such as a pay stub, benefits letter, or bank statement.
Before you start
• Read these instructions and the whole form before responding to any questions.
• Be honest. Lying to the court is against the law.
• Fill in every question. If you need more space, include “continued on additional page” next to the
question on the form. On the additional page, include the question number and finish your
response. Include your name and case number on each additional page. Attach the additional
pages to your request.
This page contains instructions only—do not file.
